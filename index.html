      A balanced 40-minute session

      Show the same task. Ask before assessing.

      1. 5 min: explain that these are animated proposals. Describe the actual robot configurations and whether future demonstrations are scripted, teleoperated or autonomous.
      2. 20 min: compare steps, reception, handover, flat-floor guidance, emergency boundaries and a shared workflow.
      3. 10 min: repeat two scenes with changed conditions: add a G1 display, remove its gripper, or provide an accessible route.
      4. 5 min: ask which service is worth building and whether a person or simpler tool is better.

      Do not describe Pepper as universally friendly or G1 as universally capable. Separate interest, usefulness, comfort and trust.

      Bodies remain visible, so this is not a blinded comparison. Alternate scene and panel order across groups.

      Keep possibility separate from proof.

      Every scene is an authored animation. Walking, grasping, speech, human reactions and successful outcomes shown here are illustrative. No simulation of these pictures can establish that your robot will perform the task.

      G1: edition and hardware matter

      Unitree lists G1 EDU development support and optional dexterous hands. Battery life is advertised at about two hours; arm load is posture-dependent. Some sample functions are still being developed. Confirm the delivered motion and developer interfaces.

      Pepper: a useful interface, with limits

      Pepper’s built-in tablet can support visual and touch interaction alongside speech and gestures. Its guide specifies smooth, flat ground and avoiding stairs, slopes, carpets and rugs. Social preference, empathy and learning benefit must be studied rather than inferred from its appearance.

      The European Pepper page now redirects to Maxtronics. Its notice states that cloud services end on 1 October 2026 and there is currently no official European support centre.
